Ten-Item Personality Inventory (TIPI)

Your Big Five personality profile

Rate yourself on 10 short descriptions (about 2 minutes) to see where you fall on five broad personality traits compared with a large group of other people.

Your privacy

  • Your answers are never saved or sent anywhere. Your answers are scored right here in your web browser, using percentile tables prepared in advance from the published norms. Your answers, age range, and gender never leave your device, and they're erased when you close or reload this page.
  • This app doesn't ask for your name, email, or student ID, and it doesn't use cookies, tracking, or analytics.
  • This web page is delivered by GitHub Pages (a service of GitHub, Inc.), which automatically records the IP address of every device that loads it, for security purposes. That record doesn't include anything you enter in this app.
  • By using this app, you consent to this.

3. Your results

Each point shows your percentile. The dashed ring is the 50th percentile (the comparison group's average); the outer edge is 100.

Traits are shown in OCEAN order. The TIPI measures Emotional Stability, the reverse of Neuroticism (the N in OCEAN), so it appears in N’s place: a high Emotional Stability score corresponds to low Neuroticism.

How to read these results
  • A percentile is the estimated percentage of the comparison group who scored below you, with people who gave exactly your score counted as half below and half above. The 50th percentile is average.
  • Low (15th percentile or below), Medium low (16th–49th), Medium high (50th–83rd), and High (84th or above) split the range at one standard deviation below the average, the average, and one standard deviation above it.
  • Higher isn't better. Each trait has advantages and disadvantages at both ends.
  • This is a quick snapshot. The TIPI uses only two items per trait, so your scores could shift a bit if you took it again. For more on the TIPI’s reliability, see Sam Gosling’s note on alpha reliability and factor structure (opens in a new tab).
  • Percentiles are estimates. They're calculated from the comparison group's average and standard deviation, assuming scores follow a bell curve. That's least accurate for scores near the ends of the 1–7 scale, so treat very high and very low results as approximate.
  • About the norms. The comparison data come from about 278,000 people aged 15 and older who took the TIPI online (Gosling et al., 2014; see About the TIPI below). In the full online sample, more than half lived in the United States and nearly three-quarters identified as White, so the norms may fit some groups better than others.

About the TIPI

The Ten-Item Personality Inventory was developed by Samuel Gosling, Jason Rentfrow, and William Swann (2003). To learn more about it, visit Sam Gosling’s TIPI page (opens in a new tab).
